A pop-up shouldn’t get to decide it’s important.

Ad Tray catches pop-up ads before the browser ever makes a tab. Each one loads in a small window in the corner instead, so you can see what it actually is and then open it, or not.

Blockers destroy the pop-up. Ad Tray keeps it, silently, where it can’t interrupt you. Those are different promises.

What happens when a site tries to open one

  1. The request is cancelled

    Ad Tray intercepts the call the page makes to open a window. The browser is never asked for a tab, so nothing appears in your tab strip and nothing steals focus.

  2. The page loads in the tray

    The target renders inside a small sandboxed frame in the corner. It can’t open more pop-ups, navigate the page you’re on, or reach its own cookies.

  3. You decide

    Open it in a real tab, dismiss it, or trust the site so its pop-ups pass through from then on. Logins and payment windows are trusted out of the box.

What it isn’t

Not an antivirus

It can’t see your files or your system. Nothing outside the browser is in its reach, and any extension claiming otherwise is overselling.

Not an ad blocker

Banners and in-page ads are untouched. Ad Tray only deals with windows that try to open on their own.

Not always going to render

Some pages refuse to display in a frame or need cookies the sandbox withholds. Those show an empty preview, and the open button still works.

Not collecting anything

There’s no account, no server, and no telemetry. Your held pop-ups and trusted sites stay in your browser.

A calyx is the ring of sepals that wraps a flower bud and holds it closed until it’s ready to open. It isn’t a wall. It’s a pause.

That’s the idea behind the software I want to build here: tools that sit between you and something demanding your attention, and hand the timing back to you. Ad Tray is the first one, and it’s small on purpose.
